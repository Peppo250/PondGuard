# M6.3 — pH Analog Front-End

## Objective

Implement a high-impedance pH interface that is compatible with the
single-supply ESP32/ADS1115 system and explicitly handles the bipolar nature
of a raw pH electrode.

## Critical design correction

The earlier one-op-amp `AD8603 buffer only` concept is insufficient for the
recommended single-supply level-shift topology.

A raw pH electrode is bipolar and high impedance. Therefore M6.3 uses a
**dual-channel AD8607**:
- channel A = buffered 1.65 V bias
- channel B = high-impedance pH measurement buffer

The two-channel arrangement is preferred over pretending a single AD8603
can simultaneously create the bias and buffer the electrode.

## Signal chain

```text
3.3V
 ↓
10k/10k divider
 ↓
AD8607A
 ↓
VPH_BIAS = 1.65V
 ↓
pH reference electrode

pH measuring electrode
 ↓
AD8607B
 ↓
VPH_SIGNAL
 ↓
ADS1115 AIN0+

ADS1115 AIN3- = VPH_BIAS
 ↓
VPH_DIFF
 ↓
ESP32-C3
```

## Why this is appropriate

Analog Devices documents AD8603 as a pH-probe buffer because typical pH
electrodes have very high source resistance, and it highlights guarding and
shielding at the high-impedance input.

Texas Instruments' pH-electrode design guidance explicitly states that the
pH signal is bipolar, requires level shifting in a single-supply system, and
needs a high-input-impedance buffer.

## Nernst model

At 25 °C:

`S = 2.303RT/F ≈ 59.16 mV/pH`

Centered around pH 7:

`VPH_DIFF ≈ S(T) × (7 - pH)`

The sign must be verified with the physical probe.

## ADC

ADS1115 is used in differential mode:
- AIN0 = buffered measuring electrode
- AIN3 = buffered electrode bias
- I²C → ESP32-C3

A first candidate PGA range is ±0.512 V because it is large enough for the
approximate 0–14 pH theoretical span while giving substantially finer
resolution than a wide ±4.096 V range.

The actual probe and operating temperature must be measured before freezing
the PGA configuration.

## Calibration

Minimum recommended:
- pH 7 buffer
- pH 4 or pH 10 buffer

Store slope/offset calibration coefficients.

Use DS18B20 temperature data to compensate the theoretical electrode slope
and, where needed, calibration buffers' temperature dependence.

## Layout

The pH electrode input is a high-impedance analog node. Keep it away from:
- GSM
- switching regulators
- SSR/control lines
- high-current pump traces
- digital clocks

Use short traces, clean board surface, guarding/shielding as appropriate,
and high-insulation construction.

## Acceptance criteria

- dual-buffer topology documented
- 1.65 V bias buffered
- differential ADC interface documented
- temperature-dependent Nernst calculation implemented
- calibration strategy documented
- preliminary SPICE/KiCad artifacts present
- no field accuracy claim
