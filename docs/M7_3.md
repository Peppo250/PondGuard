# PondGuard M7.3 — Parametric Sweeps & Pass/Fail Boundary Analysis

## Purpose

M7.3 extends M7.2 from nominal electrical calculations to deterministic worst-case and Monte-Carlo-style sensitivity analysis. It remains a simulation-only milestone: no physical hardware or field validation is implied.

## Analyses

- DO TIA: ±5% resistor/capacitor sweep, cutoff and 3 µA output gain.
- pH AFE: temperature sweep from -10 °C to 50 °C across pH 4–10; checks ADS1115 ±0.512 V range.
- NH₃ input: ±5% series resistor, capacitor, and ADC-side load sweep; checks loaded cutoff boundary.
- Battery divider: ±1% resistor tolerance and 6.0–8.4 V battery sweep; checks ADC headroom.
- Power budget: 6.0–8.4 V battery and 85/90/95% converter-efficiency sensitivity; checks selected 3 A rail-current envelope.
- GSM bulk capacitance: 2 A idealized pulse boundary analysis for 0.1–2 ms; identifies the capacitance needed to limit a 4.0 V rail to ≥3.4 V.

## Acceptance interpretation

Pass/fail limits in this milestone are engineering screening boundaries, not manufacturer guarantees. Vendor transient behavior, regulator control-loop response, ESR/ESL, battery impedance, PCB parasitics, and op-amp/ADC macromodel behavior are not fully represented.

### Key findings

- DO TIA ±5% passive sweep: 30.72–37.47 Hz; all 5,000 samples stayed inside the 29–39 Hz screening window.
- pH envelope: maximum absolute differential output is 192.39 mV, comfortably inside the ±0.512 V ADS1115 full-scale range.
- NH₃ filter ±5% sweep: 1.460–1.777 kHz; all 5,000 samples stayed inside the 1.4–1.8 kHz screening window.
- Battery divider: maximum simulated ADC node is 2.111 V over the 6.0–8.4 V and ±1% resistor sweep.
- Combined nominal output demand remains 15.64 W. At 6.0 V battery and 85% efficiency, the sensitivity model reaches 3.067 A battery input current. This is a system-level budget result, not a BMS/pack approval.
- A 2 A, 1 ms pulse starting at 4.0 V requires about 3,333 µF in an ideal capacitor-only model to stay at or above 3.4 V. The existing M7.2 2,200 µF example would droop by about 0.91 V in that idealized case. Practical selection must include ESR and regulator transient response; 4,700 µF is a candidate, not a frozen requirement.
